Here is a question buyers rarely ask before ordering, and regret later: **when this generator breaks in two years, who fixes it — and how fast?**

It is an especially important question if the set is going to another country. Let's talk about it straight.

## The two ways a generator "breaks"

Most of what a backup generator needs is not dramatic failure. It is ordinary wear: an air filter clogs, an oil change is due, a sensor gives a fault, a starter motor gets tired. All normal, all expected.

The question is: can your local team get those parts quickly, and does a technician there know the engine? On a common, widely-built engine, yes. On a niche one, every service visit becomes a special order with a wait.

## Why the engine brand decides your repair options

Every generator is a box of someone else's engine. The canopy and controller you can change later. The engine is fixed. So before you buy, ask one thing: **in the country where this machine will live, are these engines common?**

This is exactly why buyers on export projects often land on a Perkins-powered set. The engines are built in huge numbers and supported through a broad, international network. That means filters, sensors and injectors are standard parts, and local technicians already know how to work on them.

## What this means for you in practice

- **Service day** — a local technician shows up with the right consumables, no lead time.
- **A fault at 2 a.m.** — you are not waiting weeks for an imported spare.
- **Ten years of ownership** — predictable, affordable maintenance instead of surprises.

## When you don't need to think about this

If the machine stays local and your own team services it, price matters more and the network matters less. The "easy to repair abroad" case gets strongest precisely when the generator will be far from home.

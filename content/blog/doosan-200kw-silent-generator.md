If your site is a city building, a courtyard, or a refurbished office block, you have probably already hit the same wall everyone hits: you need backup power, but where on earth does the generator go? And will it be so loud that the office next door complains the first time it runs?

These are real questions, not details. Let's answer them the way a buyer needs.

## "Can I just put it outside?"

Yes — that is exactly what a **silent (canopied)** generator is for. Unlike an open set that needs its own machine room, a canopied unit is weatherproof and noise-insulated, so it sits on a concrete pad outside. No extra building, no plant room, no construction delay.

That alone removes the two biggest headaches of urban installation.

## "How loud is it, really?"

A bare diesel engine outdoors will get you a noise complaint within a week. A canopied 200 kW set drops that to around 72 dB(A) at 7 metres — comparable to a busy street rather than a machine room. In most commercial and residential surroundings, that is acceptable.

If your generator will sit close to an office window, tell us the distance. We size the canopy and confirm the noise level so you are not guessing.

## "I barely have any space — will it even fit?"

This is where the engine underneath matters. A compact, well-engineered block lets the whole finished package fit a smaller footprint. For buyers on a rooftop, a side yard or a narrow loading bay, that compactness is the difference between "it fits" and "we have to redesign".

When you ask us, tell us the actual dimensions you have — not "roughly a corner". We will tell you if the set fits before you commit to the design.

## Your siting questions, answered

| Your question | Practical answer |
|---|---|
| Does it need a machine room? | No — canopied sets live outdoors |
| Will it annoy the neighbours? | Canopied ~72 dB(A), usually acceptable |
| How much room do I need? | A compact ~200 kW canopied footprint, pad-prepared |
| Roughly how long to install? | Deliver, connect, commission — no room-building |

## When an open set is actually fine

If you are building a new plant with a dedicated, soundproof generator room far from people, an open set is cheaper and you don't need the canopy. The silent set is for the urban retrofit, the infill site, the building with no spare land — the ones where "where does it go" is the first question.

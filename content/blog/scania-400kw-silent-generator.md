Almost everyone shops a generator by purchase price. Almost everyone forgets to ask the question that actually decides the cost: **how much fuel will this thing burn, and how often will it run?**

Let's work it out together, because the answer changes which engine you should buy.

## Start with one number: how many hours will it run?

Before you compare engines, be honest about this:

- A set that runs **once a year** during a storm — fuel hardly matters. Buy the cheapest that rates.
- A set that runs **hundreds of hours a year** on a weak grid — fuel is your real budget, and efficiency matters a lot.

Same power, completely different buying decision.

## Do the rough math

Say you need 400 kW and the set burns around 80 litres an hour at typical load. Over, say, 800 hours a year, that is tens of thousands of litres. A few percentage points of better fuel use — invisible on the brochure — is a serious saving every year, and it compounds over a decade.

This is why an efficient, strong-torque engine like a Scania block earns its keep: it holds its fuel economy even at partial load, which is where standby sets actually spend most of their time.

## The traps buyers fall into

- **Oversizing** — buying a much bigger engine than you need. A big engine idling underloaded burns fuel for nothing and wears oddly.
- **Ignoring partial load** — efficiency at 100% power is nice, but you rarely run at 100%. Ask about efficiency at half load.
- **Only reading the sticker price** — the cheap engine looks great until the first fuel bill.

## The simple rule

If your generator runs a lot, buy the efficient engine. If it barely runs, don't overpay for efficiency you won't use. Either way, size it to your actual load — not a round number.

## Engineering checklist before you order

Before choosing a generator, collect the exact connected load, the largest motor kW, the site altitude, ambient temperature and the ATS logic you need. A unit sized too small will trip under step load; a unit sized too large will run at low load, wet-stack and waste fuel. Ask the supplier for a one-hour factory load test report, not just a nameplate photo.

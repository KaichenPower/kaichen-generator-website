When buyers compare large generator quotes, they usually focus on the engine brand and the price. Buried in the small print is a word that changes both: *standby* or *prime*.

The same physical 550 kW set, rated as standby or as prime, will deliver different hours of life, different warranties, and different price tags. Getting this wrong is one of the most expensive mistakes a buyer can make in the 500 kW class.

## What standby duty means

A *standby* set is designed to sit idle. Its job is to be ready, not to run.

- It runs only during outages, typically 20 to 100 hours a year.
- The engine is rated to deliver full power for that intermittent duty.
- After 500 hours it gets a minor service; after a few years it is still nearly new.
- The price reflects this lighter duty cycle.

If your grid is reliable and the set only covers rare outages, standby is the right call. You are not paying for running hours you will never use.

## What prime duty means

A *prime* set is the main power source. It runs hours every week, because there is no grid, or the grid is too weak to rely on.

- It runs 8 to 24 hours a day, weeks on end.
- The engine is derated slightly, built with heavier bearings and a larger cooling system.
- The fuel system and lubrication are specified for continuous duty.
- The warranty is longer, but the price is higher.

If your site has no grid or a grid that drops out several times a week, prime is not optional. A standby-rated set pushed into prime duty will wear out in a fraction of its expected life.

## The comparison at 550 kW

| Question | Standby rating | Prime rating |
|---|---|---|
| Annual running hours | 20–200 | 2,000–6,000 |
| Engine loading | 100% intermittently | 70–80% continuously |
| Expected engine life | 8–10 years standby | 15,000–30,000 hours |
| Price level | Lower | Higher |
| Fuel tank size needed | Day tank | Bulk tank |
| Redundancy | Often one set | Often two parallel sets |

## The trap: buying standby for prime duty

This is the mistake we see most. A buyer looks at two quotes for the "same" 550 kW set. The standby quote is noticeably cheaper. They buy it. For the first year, everything is fine. In the second year, the engine starts to use oil. In the third year, a rebuild is due — three times earlier than expected.

The cheap choice was not cheaper. It was a downpayment on a premature rebuild.

## When a 550 kW set is the right size

A 550 kW Yuchai-based silent set lands in the load band of 400 to 480 kW continuous. That covers:

- A mid-size industrial plant running a full shift.
- A large hotel or hospital complex with central cooling.
- A construction camp with heavy machinery and welfare blocks.
- A water pumping station on a weak grid.

Yuchai engines in this range are common for prime duty because the block is built for long hours, the fuel system tolerates variable diesel quality, and the service network across Asia is dense. For a remote industrial site, that combination matters.

## The question that settles it

Before you compare prices, ask yourself one thing:

*How many hours a year will this set actually run?*

If the answer is "a few dozen, only during storms," buy standby. If the answer is "several thousand, because the grid is not to be trusted," buy prime. Tell us the number and we will quote the rating that matches — not the cheaper one that will disappoint you in three years.

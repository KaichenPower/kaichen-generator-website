Most people shopping for a generator start in the wrong place. They look at price first, then power. The question that actually decides whether the machine will serve you is simpler: what exactly has to keep running when the grid goes down?

For a small business, that list is usually short. A few lights, a refrigerator, a router, a point-of-sale terminal, a couple of computers. Add it up and you land somewhere near 20 to 25 kW of continuous load. That is the moment the 30 kW silent set starts to look like the answer.

## What 30kW realistically covers

The honest way to think about it is not "what is the number on the box?" but "what survives an outage without me panicking."

- A small retail shop with refrigerated display
- A two- or three-room clinic or dental practice
- A rural office with server storage and cooling
- A small workshop running a few bench tools
- A guesthouse or small restaurant kitchen on a weak grid

Notice what is not on that list. A 30 kW set will not carry a central air-conditioning plant, a large water pump station, or a production line with multiple motors. If your load list has those, stop here and read about larger sets — you will save money by sizing up once instead of replacing a set later.

## The starting-current trap at small sizes

Sizing mistakes are not only a large-generator problem. At 30 kW they bite just as hard, because the margin is thin.

A refrigerator, an air compressor, or a water pump pulls several times its running power for the first second it switches on. On a small set that moment is when lights dim, the voltage sags, and sensitive electronics reset. If you have chosen a 30 kW set on "running load only," you may find it cannot start the very fridge you bought it to protect.

The practical fix is to list the motors separately. When you tell us "I have a 3 kW compressor, a 2 kW fridge, and ten computers," we can tell you whether the 30 kW set will start them together, or whether a slightly larger unit removes the worry for a small step in budget.

## Why buyers choose a silent canopy at this size

A 30 kW open-frame set costs less, but it belongs outside in a plant yard. A silent-canopy version costs a little more and pays you back in location freedom:

- It can sit against the wall of a shop or clinic, within a few meters of people working.
- It runs at roughly 65 to 70 dB(A) at seven meters — the level of a normal conversation, not a jackhammer.
- It is weather-sealed, so you do not need to build a dedicated machine room.

For a small business, that difference usually decides the purchase. A cheaper open set that forces you to pour a concrete pad and build a shed is not actually cheaper once installation is counted.

## A checklist before you order

- [ ] List every device that must stay on, split into running load and motor loads.
- [ ] Confirm your voltage (220V single phase, 380V three phase, or other).
- [ ] Decide whether the set is standby (only outages) or prime (long hours of weak grid).
- [ ] Check the local service network for the engine brand — a small set still needs filter and oil support.
- [ ] Plan a fuel day tank for at least eight hours of running.

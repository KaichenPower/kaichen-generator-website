You run a small factory, a workshop, or a farm. You know you need backup power. But the one number nobody will give you straight is: **how big does it actually need to be?**

Buy too big and you overspend. Buy too small and it shuts down the moment a motor switches on. Let's keep it simple.

## The two numbers to work out

Forget complicated calculations. Start with two things:

1. **What has to keep running** when the power goes out? (Not everything — just the essentials.)
2. **Which of those things are motors or pumps** that draw a big surge when they start?

List those, and you have the heart of the sizing problem.

## Why "add it all up" is not enough

Adding up the running power gets you close, but the motor surge is the trap. When a compressor or pump starts, it pulls several times its normal power for a moment. A generator sized on the running total alone will stutter on that first start — exactly when you need it most.

That is why experienced buyers either over-size slightly for motor starts, or have us look at the load list for them.

## The rule of thumb for small sites

After a few projects, people settle on simple guides:

- Run the generator at about **70–80% of its rated power** in normal use.
- A small workshop, farm or shop cluster often lands around **100–250 kVA** — which is roughly an **80–200 kW** set.
- If you are near the top of that range, a **200 kW (250 kVA)** silent set is a sensible, economical choice.

## Your sizing questions, answered

| Your question | Practical answer |
|---|---|
| How do I start? | List essentials + motors that start on surge |
| Is bigger safer? | No — oversized wastes fuel and wears oddly |
| What if I grow later? | Pick a set a little above your current load |
| Roughly where does a small site land? | Often 80–200 kW standby |

## The honest caveat

If your business is growing fast, or you will run the set for many hours a day, plan ahead — size up and look at efficiency. For ordinary backup duty on a small working site, a sturdy, economical 200 kW silent set is a measured, low-risk purchase.
